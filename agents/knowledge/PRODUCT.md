# From case to product

## One deep concept pass
Explore genuinely different core jobs internally before selecting the concept: changing the name or adding modules to the same administrative pipeline is not a different direction. Present the strongest supported concept, not the first plausible category template or a list of superficial alternatives. Keep this within one focused synthesis, without simulated panels or extra planning ceremonies.
Read the announced case and track, then understand the substantial job behind it: who initiates it, what result they need, what inputs and authority they have, which decisions and handoffs complicate it, and where current tools fail.
Start from the company's announced case and a consequential problem it actually exposes. Establish who benefits, what currently fails and the practical economic value of fixing it: avoided loss, reduced effort or a useful result that becomes attainable. A familiar SaaS category, entertaining generation or polished dashboard is not a sufficient purpose. Let the case determine the work and interface; do not default to data analysis, chat or an administrative pipeline. Explain the practical consequence of the current problem: whose work or decision suffers, what delay, loss, error or missed opportunity results, and how the product changes that outcome. Connect the implemented mechanism to this benefit through inspectable inputs and results. Distinguish measured effects from expected benefits; never invent time savings or impact figures.
Separate mandatory case behavior from examples and optional directions. Identify the costly decision or operation in the user's actual workflow, then build the connected capabilities needed to complete it. Validate the hardest assumption against available source material or a real user's workflow; do not mistake a familiar product category for the problem.
Use the supplied case and working examples first. If a crucial domain fact is unknown, make a bounded investigation of real workflow material. Do not invent simplistic stories to fit a feature list or produce a broad market-research report.

Follow the difficult operation through to the user's next consequential action. An assessment needs traceable reasons, correction controls and a route to the actual decision; generated material needs the editing or execution that makes it usable. Human review is valuable for consequential judgments: provide evidence and ways to correct or revise the result where warranted, without gating every action or imposing one fixed route. Choose that endpoint from the case, not from a generic feature menu. Keep judgments, recommendations and executed changes distinct. Demonstrate benefit against the original task with inspectable inputs/results; an internal score or a claimed accuracy percentage is not independent evidence of improvement.

MAIN considers these questions in one synthesis, without assembling a simulated panel:
- Value: is the difficulty real, consequential and frequent enough to justify this product? What work actually disappears or becomes possible?
- Reach: does the connected suite handle the meaningful variations of this problem, incomplete inputs, revisions and follow-through?
- Distinction: what visible, credible and useful advantage is likely missing from obvious AI solutions to this same case? Compare genuinely different approaches internally and demonstrate the selected mechanism; novelty claims, unusual gimmicks and a mandatory market survey are not evidence of value.
- Feasibility: can the available data, credentials and tools perform that mechanism within the available time? What must be tried first?
- Engineering and operations: where do permissions, consistency, latency, provider cost and recovery matter for this use?
- Experience: can a new user start immediately, understand each result and refine it without learning the internal complexity?
Resolve consequential weaknesses before proposing the product. Keep the actual decisions, not a transcript of this reasoning or a scorecard.

Propose one strong concept in Russian:
1. A memorable name made from two meaningful English words joined in PascalCase, chosen after the distinctive mechanism is clear. Compare several different naming directions internally, including less obvious associations. Reject default combinations such as StudioForge, CanvasForge and StudioFlow, and mechanically swapping Studio/Canvas/Forge/Flow/Lab/Hub. Do not replace familiarity with an unpronounceable or meaningless rarity. The final choice should be easy to say, remember and explain; do not claim trademark/domain uniqueness without checking.
2. A short Russian slogan, one-sentence product description and original logo proposal with a visible sketch/SVG, its meaning, silhouette and small-size/monochrome use. Tie the identity to the distinctive benefit.
3. A concrete explanation: user, task, important inputs and usable output.
4. A connected capability package and how its shared data/results flow.
5. The necessary AI mechanism, real tools and source inputs.
6. Several independently useful user jobs supported by shared capabilities, plus one concrete journey showing how operations combine. A fixed pipeline's successive steps are not multiple jobs. Use plain Russian: the actual input, what the user asks, what the system does and the usable result. Label illustrative examples as examples; do not invent a named persona's historical projects, prices, rights issues or business facts to manufacture credibility.
7. The practical distinction and any dependency that materially changes feasibility.

Wait for user approval of concept, identity and scope. Record approval and corrections in PROJECT. The existing technical foundation is already authorized; product implementation and implementation delegation begin after this approval.
After approval, create the detailed WORKFLOW plan once and execute it without a second approval. Ask about a genuine missing decision rather than silently changing the product promise.

## Ambition and coherence
Rubric compliance is the floor. Aim for a competitive product whose central mechanism, useful breadth, reliability and original presentation make its advantage visible. Design for real adoption and extension with clear boundaries, not speculative infrastructure. A generic AI wrapper, cloned category dashboard or copied design-study logo/composition does not meet this standard.
Make the interface self-explanatory: recognizable task language, one clear starting action, meaningful defaults, contextual controls and clear feedback. A user should understand what can be done, what changed and how to continue without knowing the internal architecture. Preserve inputs across navigation and errors; reveal advanced controls when useful rather than making them prerequisites.
The product should let its intended user accomplish a substantial result that was previously beyond their practical skill, time or tool access. A multimodal model, an agent loop and many tools are implementation ingredients, not the distinctive value by themselves. Establish the concrete outcome and why the complete execution makes it attainable; do not confuse novelty in the architecture with value to the user.

Design the first session around a useful, convincing result within five minutes of entry, with minimal user effort. Define in the approved concept: the starting material, the smallest necessary user action, the resulting usable artifact/domain change, and the one refinement the user can make immediately. Count onboarding, uploads, setup and provider waiting in this time. Do not front-load project configuration, provider keys, a long questionnaire or tool selection. Use sensible defaults and offer a clearly labelled runnable example when the user has no material; examples must execute the real capability, not swap in a canned success. Request only information that actually blocks a sound result. If the heavy job takes longer, deliver a genuinely usable part within that interval and continue transparently; a spinner, progress message or teaser is not the result.

Five minutes is an internal acceptance target to verify with a fresh-user scenario, not a marketing claim until measured. “Magic”, “superpowers”, “weeks in seconds”, comparisons to ordinary AI and agent/harness terminology are not landing-page copy. Show precisely what the user can make, change or achieve and the output quality that supports it.

Start from the work the intended user actually wants finished. For a creator, this may be producing or transforming the creative material itself; do not automatically divert the concept into budgets, calendars, contracts and preparation around that work. Administrative workflows are justified only when the selected case makes them the actual problem.
Test the value against a general assistant supplied with the same files: what concrete operations, precise edits, execution/rendering, inspection or usable outputs does this product perform for the user? Loading the user's data, remembering a project and generating several texts are baseline conveniences, not sufficient distinction on their own. Name and demonstrate the actual internal capability that removes manual work. Avoid absolute claims that another assistant could never do it.
Solve the whole relevant problem through a package of useful functions, tools, integrations and customization. Shared context should remove repeated entry and movement between disconnected services.
There is no one-feature target or arbitrary feature cap. The approved package is the commitment. Each capability should complete a relevant user job or materially improve another job's result. Make the product's advantage understandable through one representative journey, while keeping its other useful scenarios complete and accessible. In the first few minutes, a newcomer should understand the problem, see a credible result and recognize why this approach is useful compared with the obvious alternative.
AI is mandatory and should perform a central share of the domain work through an actual harness: choose tools, observe results, continue from persistent state and create usable artifacts or domain changes. Read HARNESS.md for contracts and concrete depth tests. Interpretation, extraction, evidence gathering, generation and comparison should connect to real operations and follow-through. Exact calculations, permissions and supported mutations remain ordinary code. Match autonomy to the case: automate substantive work while preserving any required expert decision, with evidence and meaningful correction controls. Deliver the first useful result before optional expensive detail when these can be separated without losing correctness.
Users should receive results they can use now, refine on the platform and carry into the next action. A generic answer paragraph or static display is inadequate when the job needs an editable document, decision, artifact or executable workflow.
The home screen offers useful jobs or lets the user state a goal. Design a shared workspace around real materials and results; the agent chooses tools/procedures and handles intermediate work. Do not turn each internal tool or pipeline step into a compulsory page or require the user to move outputs manually through a wizard. Show meaningful progress, editable results and necessary choices in place. Separate pages are for distinct destinations the user needs, not the backend execution graph. Charts belong only where actual users need actual measurements.
Build the full ru/kk/en interface. AI follows the user naturally, without generating three copies of their content merely because the interface has three languages.
Apply strong functionality, code quality, performance and design together. A beautiful incomplete demo and a powerful unusable tool both miss the objective.
Deliver a finished, deployable product with real integrations and the full approved functionality. Preserve the useful first-session result, depth, clarity, visual quality and credible sales presentation. Do not replace unfinished functionality with technical demo notices. Apply ENGINEERING.md's reliability requirements and COPY.md's presentation rules.
Monetization is outside this product scope. Do not add paid subscriptions, pricing sections, checkout, paid feature gates or promotional codes. Make the complete approved functionality directly usable; retain ordinary permissions and provider-cost safeguards. Do not mention absent or future monetization in the interface or replace it with placeholders.

## Implementation sequence
Reuse the prepared stack. Establish canonical domain records, contracts, UI primitives and chosen product tokens. Probe the hardest real AI/data path early.
Connect one meaningful end-to-end slice to expose integration problems, then complete the remaining suite in parallel where independent. Keep adding real shared operations rather than separate fake demonstrations.
Resolve unavailable integrations or discuss a material scope change. Do not substitute canned outcomes or label unfinished features complete.
Finish the full workflows, persistence, recovery, adaptive screens and visual refinement. Then execute the dedicated code review/stability stage in WORKFLOW.md, fix its findings and verify affected scenarios before the final README and video stages. Reuse prepared Docker/manual SSH delivery; MAIN deploys completed blocks with the configured checkpoint. The expected five-hour implementation window should include this review and final media. Use a small shared set of reproducible case scenarios to verify the mechanism, seed a useful quick-start and capture real demo video material, without substituting canned model outputs.

## A landing page that earns the next action
Carry the selected example or entered material from the landing action through sign-in and required account connections into the matching first operation. Preserve that context so the user can reach the promised result without finding the capability again or re-entering the same information. Respect authentication and ownership throughout.
The landing page must sell the actual product as well as meet the accepted visual quality. Its opening makes the intended user recognize a relevant task, understand the concrete result and see convincing product output. The main CTA starts the shortest path to that result. Support the promise with a legible example of real input becoming useful output, then explain the few capabilities and meaningful variations needed to understand the value. Resolve actual adoption questions such as required material, control over revisions and where the output can be used. Finish with the same clear action.

Choose the page's composition and section order from this argument; do not fill a default hero/features/FAQ template or paste the product's navigation into feature cards. Show finished work and consequential edits at useful scale. Use real evidence; do not invent testimonials, metrics, time savings, customer logos or commercial plans. Typography, motion and illustration direct attention to the promise and its proof. The landing CTA and demo video CTA should lead to the first-session capability described above. Read design/COPY.md for authored copy rules. After the final README and video are complete, integrate the demo into the landing composition with a useful poster, lazy loading and accessible playback controls. Start playback on user intent; do not autoplay sound or let the video delay the primary action.
