# Product artwork and demo video

Use after the product's purpose and visual system are defined. MAIN can assign a video/artwork worker a bounded task with exact output paths, approved behavior, design tokens and available real media. That worker cannot create agents.

## Product demo video
A complete, roughly one-minute product demo video is required near completion: normally 55–70 seconds, 1920×1080. Its job is to make someone understand the product's idea, positioning and useful outcome, want that outcome and know the next action. Give it an independently authored scenario and visual staging. Neither a design-system showcase, a moving version of the landing page nor a chronological screen tour satisfies it. Finish product code review/stability and the final README before this stage.
Build the visual spine as a continuous chain: overview → isolate a meaningful UI object → push in → lift/transform that object → assemble the next working surface around it → reveal the changed result → follow the next object. Each scene grows from something already visible in the previous scene. Preserve object identity, spatial direction and momentum while changing scale and surrounding context. Layered panels and parallax support this continuity; alternate the specific transformations so the chain does not become a repeated trick. The cursor appears only when it clarifies an input or consequence, rather than leading every shot. Camera movement remains motivated and settles for reading/contact; continuity does not require constant slow drifting.
Before designing shots, read design/video/ANALYSIS.md for visual storytelling and staging, then the relevant mechanisms in design/video/RECIPES.md. Apply them to the actual product and its approved visual identity.
No voiceover. Mix music and effects as one coherent soundtrack. Effects should make actions tangible without repeatedly pulling attention away from the visual story or overpowering the music. Adjust individual cue levels and soften harsh transients; duck music only where an important cue would otherwise be masked. Use a small varied sound palette matched to actions—contact, typing, movement, surface changes and completion—instead of reusing one sound for everything. Give significant presses, releases, panel lifts, object handoffs, fast travel and result reveals appropriate tactile or airy accents. Review these actions for missing cues rather than sounding only a few transitions; vary and group cues so overlapping movement remains clear. Use local sound assets with usable rights and record their provenance. The demo video must communicate its value muted.
Choose sound by its actual timbre, not its filename: short dry tactile clicks, restrained typing texture and brief airy whooshes that follow acceleration/travel. Avoid piercing tonal beeps, repeated notification pings, electrical buzz and sustained droning backgrounds. A sine tone named click.wav or whoosh.wav is not a suitable substitute. Choose a light musical bed with phrasing and space; do not synthesize a static oscillator chord to fill the minute. Audition assets individually and in a rendered representative passage at ordinary listening volume before reusing them. Align impacts to contact and movement sounds to motion; do not attach a sound to every appearing label. Judge the exported mix, not just source gain numbers, and fix masking, harshness and abrupt endings.
Start from a one-sentence concrete product promise and its strongest verified example. Decide what the viewer must understand and want; then select visual proof. The first few seconds should establish an identifiable job/result, not colors, typography or abstract materials. Reveal the desired result early, show the small input/action that makes it possible, dramatize the consequential transformation and refinement, show useful breadth or follow-through, and finish with the product name and a concrete CTA. This is an adaptable narrative, not a required count of scenes or a walkthrough of every intermediate step. Several related use cases can support one positioning idea. Edit out routine navigation and waiting.
The start of an audio clip is not necessarily its audible impact. Inspect leading silence, attack and tail: align the actual click transient with contact and the whoosh's strongest movement with the fastest travel. Trim a local asset when needed and preserve a natural ending. Verify the exported passage with its music; equal gain values do not imply equal perceived loudness.

Before writing the animation timeline, make one concise beat-to-shot plan. For each beat state: what the viewer now understands, the short authored line if needed, the actual input/output that proves it, the shot composition, motivated cursor/camera action, continuity object, sound cue and duration. If the meaning column says only “show dashboard”, “show card”, “demonstrate depth” or “show component”, the beat lacks a product message. Write new concise demo video copy for this story; do not copy the site's section headings into successive video scenes. Do not show internal harness/tool architecture in place of the outcome.

Map each product beat to a visible operation: a small input proves the easy start, a transformed editable result proves useful output, and saving or downstream use proves follow-through. Define the shot size, focal hierarchy, action timing, spatial continuity and energy in the same shot plan.

Compose for video: alternate full-frame output, readable macro detail, moving type, spatially arranged meaningful objects and selected UI surfaces. Keep one dominant subject per beat. A wide shot earns its place by showing useful context; do not leave the action tiny in a mostly empty 1920×1080 frame. Use a camera push when attention moves to an operation, then let its transformed result carry the next composition. Include a representative connected 8–12-second passage early, verify both its message and motion against that shot's intended message and physical handoff, then reuse it in the final minute. If the passage still fails, change its construction before extending it to a minute.
Effective interface animation uses elements that respond to input, expand, change state, move between depth planes and become the next working surface. Preserve that cause/effect chain. A zoom over an unchanged screenshot, a title swap or a page transition alone does not satisfy this direction.

Visual pacing is an independent requirement, even when the sales story is sound. Build evolving continuous shots with successive events: objects arrive/rearrange, text types or transforms, cursor approaches and presses, a response expands, the camera follows the selected result, and that result moves into the next scene. Maintain a readable sequence of meaningful actions. The target is frequent meaningful changes within a connected composition, not rapid unrelated cuts or random motion. Long 5–8-second holds with only drifting/rotating decoration are unacceptable outside a justified reading/ending beat. Once an action or result is understood, move into the next meaningful transformation. Shorten idle entrances, pauses and exits; overlap outgoing and incoming motion around a continuing object. Alternate close detail, spatial transitions and wider results more frequently, while keeping the important change readable. Increasing frame rate or speeding up the whole timeline does not replace richer choreography.
One screen with changing camera positions cannot carry the whole minute. Even a product with one main workspace has distinct inputs, operations, editable results and downstream uses: stage those verified states as different compositions, connected by a continuing object. Alternate scale, depth, movement direction and rhythm while preserving the causal story. If source material is sparse, find meaningful transformations within the actual capability; do not pad the runtime with repeated flyovers or invent extra product features. Judge energy across the opening, middle and ending, not only the strongest final passage.

At each scene boundary specify the physical handoff: which object continues, its screen position/size/direction, what exits, what enters, and how the camera redirects attention. Use selected-object extraction, zoom-through, surface expansion, match movement or coordinated entry/exit as the story requires. Repeated fade-to-empty followed by independent entrances creates a slideshow. Do not repair it by speeding up the same slides. Rebuild a representative passage with actual choreography and compare the exported movement directly before extending it.
Use actual UI recordings, selected real components with verified state, or real exported artifacts as source material. This does not require keeping the page layout: crop, isolate, scale and arrange meaningful content for the demo video. Reuse accurate component internals when needed, but construct a demo video composition around the message. Do not animate a component gallery because components are convenient to extract. Keep the cursor attached to targets through camera transforms. A replay for the demo can compress waiting and replay a verified result; it cannot invent an unimplemented operation. Targeted capture for video material is allowed when needed; it is not a routine browser QA loop.

A landing-page 3D decoration copied unchanged and placed beside a headline is not an authored demo scene. Study the working site to understand the capability, then choose material for the planned action. Reusing a screen is useful when the camera focuses on a control, the control changes state, the selected element grows above the retained interface and then travels into the next composition. The code reuse serves that construction. Decorative assets need new staging and animation justified by the story; copying their existing idle loop does not complete the shot.

## Build the demo in deliberate passages
Do not write and render the entire minute in one pass. First study the product and select the verified input/output that conveys its value. Write the single scenario/shot plan above. For each passage, resolve the actual second-by-second events, positions, camera poses, text, sound and incoming/outgoing object before implementing it. Use labels and local shot functions on the composition timeline so one passage can be refined without rewriting the minute.

Construct and inspect the representative 8–12-second connected passage, then continue through coherent scene groups. Watch each group's exported motion at normal speed, repair weak action/framing/timing and its joining transition, then assemble it into the master. Finish with an end-to-end viewing of the complete demo and audio. This is iterative production of one required demo, not separate style-test videos, extra plans or approval requests. Preserve successful passages and refine their joins. A render command succeeding is not evidence that a scene meets the visual and storytelling requirements.

For implementation, read only the relevant section of design/video/RECIPES.md: camera journey, target zoom, typing/cursor, panel expansion, selected-element extraction, parallax unzoom or depth flight. ANALYSIS.md explains how motion, typography and composition communicate value.
The same file includes camera tracking along typed text, hover elevation with retained interface layers, press/rebound and coordinate verification. Choose and fully choreograph the mechanisms that serve each beat. A few generic camera moves over otherwise unchanged UI are insufficient. Before extending a passage, verify actual cursor contact, focal framing, meaningful response, layer continuity and sound quality as well as its message.

## Hyperframes essentials
The video project belongs in video/ and remains separate from the application dependencies and Docker build. Use the installed/pinned CLI; avoid skill installation.
A composition needs an ID, dimensions and duration. Timed elements use class="clip", data-start, data-duration and data-track-index. Register a paused GSAP timeline in window.__timelines under its composition ID. The renderer seeks to explicit times, so animation must be deterministic rather than relying on a live timer.
Hyperframes 0.8.33 is prepared. From the workspace root: pnpm --dir video run dev; pnpm --dir video run check; pnpm --dir video run render --output renders/product.mp4. Only runtime configuration, local GSAP and fonts remain in video/. Create video/index.html for the real demo video after the product exists. If creating a new project, use init with --example blank --skip-skills --skip-transcribe. Validate the composition before final MP4 rendering and inspect exported motion/audio, including the complete CTA ending. A still contact sheet alone is insufficient. Keep render logs in a file and return the output path.
Finish the demo video only after its depicted features work. MAIN integrates the final asset, gives it a poster and accessible controls, and keeps page loading light. Do not autoplay audible music.

Detailed video guidance belongs in the video owner's context, not unrelated backend/product assignments.
