# Visual system
## Intended impression
Soft, alive, dimensional and clear. The design should feel made for this product: rounded surfaces, confident readable type, controlled light and a memorable subject. Use the accepted Onest, porcelain/ink/teal palette, geometry and tokens in SYSTEM.md. Adapt the subject and composition to the product while preserving this visual contract.
Objects should appear to occupy real space; light organizes the page; large shapes and quiet areas keep the composition readable.

## Typography and proportions
Use the approved Onest typography and proportions from SYSTEM.md. Check consistent Latin, Russian and Kazakh glyphs, including Ә Ғ Қ Ң Ө Ұ Ү Һ І. Respect COPY.md bans. Establish proportions through actual components and responsive layouts with longer translated labels. Preserve the user's preference for rounded, comfortable, dimensional interfaces.
## Light and material
Choose a common light direction, typically above-left. Make three cues agree: a bright upper edge, a broad soft lower shadow and a small darker contact shadow nearest the object. The contact shadow gives weight; the broad shadow gives separation.
White cards on pale surfaces need slight value differences and selective edge highlights. Avoid turning every surface into a gray embossed button. Fields must remain recognizable as fields.
Use the accepted porcelain/off-white base, dark ink and teal accent. Where SYSTEM.md uses a dark section, preserve its deep ink, readable content planes and controlled light. Accent belongs to actions and meaningful scene details; color is not proof of quality.
Use glass on a few layers with a reason to see what is behind them: floating navigation, an overlay, a hero object. Begin with sufficiently opaque content surfaces, a faint rim and restrained blur. Reading panels stay calm and legible. A transparent rectangle with blur alone does not create convincing glass.
For sculptural refracted glass, use a high-quality rendered asset or real 3D when needed. CSS can provide light, layering and simple perspective; do not spend the build fighting a poor imitation of a complex render.
Give related illustrations the same camera angle, light direction, bevel softness, material and shadow treatment. Use fewer better objects rather than filling every card with a different stock icon.

## Hero and scene
First choose a subject tied to the actual capability: an arrangement transforming, routes resolving, layers being compared, a document becoming usable output. The subject should explain or embody the product, not be a generic AI brain or particle globe.
A strong desktop composition may place the message and usable first action in the quiet left/center area, with one larger dimensional subject to the side or below. Smaller objects support the subject at different depths. Leave clear air around text.
Build the scene as a far light field, a middle plane/object and a nearer focal object/contact shadow. An object can bridge the hero and the next section so the page feels continuous. Clip only the decorative layer where needed, not all foreground objects at each section edge.
A soft curved section boundary can extend the scene; it should not become a repeated wave separator. On mobile retain the message/action and the main object; simplify secondary pieces instead of shrinking an entire desktop collage.
The application can start with a compact hero and its real input/action. Do not force users through a long marketing page to reach the function.

## Background recipes
Choose one relevant recipe, adapted to the product:
- **Luminous planes:** two or three broad translucent vertical/diagonal planes with a softened light source behind them. A slow light shift changes their edge highlights. This gives the visible movement of light columns without neon overload.
- **Airy depth:** a nearly neutral field, a wide faint colored light wash near the focal subject and a soft floor shadow. Move a reflection or shaped light band across the subject, not random colored blobs behind the text.
- **Orbital composition:** a few topic-relevant objects follow wide quiet arcs around a stable focal area. Layer size, blur and occlusion imply depth. Motion is slow coordinated travel, not a cloud of unrelated icons.
- **Material close-up:** one glass/ceramic/ribbon-like object partly leaves the frame or crosses a section boundary. A moving highlight reveals its curvature; the text region stays quiet.

One ambient scene can contain coordinated layers. The rest of the screen supports it. Keep the background softer than the foreground and the foreground less important than the user's current action.

## Constructing an original dimensional subject
Choose one product-relevant transformation and sketch its silhouette before adding effects. Build a simple scene from a base plane, two or three shaped layers and one focal object. Give a plate visible side faces or extruded thickness, a rounded edge and a top highlight. Rotate related layers within one shared perspective; overlap and partial occlusion must agree with their depth. Put the contact shadow on the receiving surface, not arbitrarily behind the entire scene.
Use different surface values for top, side and inset faces. A moving narrow highlight can reveal curvature; it does not replace geometry. For glass, preserve a readable rim and objects visible through it, with restrained distortion and reflections from the common light source. Complex refraction is better represented by suitable rendered artwork or real 3D than many blurred CSS rectangles.
Connect motion to the capability: layers align, an object unfolds, a path resolves or a result assembles. Keep one readable resting composition and make the transformation understandable without a caption. Test the silhouette at mobile size and simplify secondary geometry. A coherent light/material system makes several original scenes feel related without reusing the same decorative object everywhere.
