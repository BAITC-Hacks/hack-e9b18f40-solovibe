# Concrete language and visual bans

Explain the actual user's task and result. Name the object and action: what someone enters, changes, compares, receives or exports. A product description should make the experience imaginable without requiring a list of technologies.
Write naturally in Russian, Kazakh or English for the active interface. Use clear verbs, meaningful headings and readable sentences. Remove claims and phrases that could describe any SaaS. Do not replace substance with "revolutionary", "powerful", "seamless", "all your tools in one place" or a stream of sentence fragments.
A button names the action. A helpful error says what failed and how to proceed. Empty states explain a real next step. Keep original user content unchanged when displaying it.

For the landing page, write a concrete sales argument: relevant task and desired result, visible proof from the working product, meaningful capabilities/variations, answers to real adoption doubts, and a CTA that begins that result. The hero must explain what someone can accomplish and show the output, not announce the existence of an AI platform. Do not expose internal harness/tools/subagent terminology unless it helps an actual user choice. Avoid “magic”, “superpowers”, generic AI comparisons or unmeasured time-saving promises. The internal first-five-minutes goal belongs in product acceptance, not automatically in marketing. See PRODUCT.md for landing/first-session contracts.

## Absolute authored-presentation bans
- Em dashes and en dashes in authored UI/marketing/README copy.
- Any eyebrow/kicker above a heading, including hero pill badges, category labels and versions. No "BETA", "INVITE-ONLY", numbered labels or "00 / INDEX".
- Numbered section eyebrows anywhere, decorative mono-caps strips and centered-dot/middle-dot word separators.
- Blue-purple gradients, AI-purple branding, mesh/blob gradient backgrounds, gold branding and black/gold combinations.
- Serif display typography, Playfair Display, Instrument Serif and Space Grotesk. No monospace UI, ornate/handwritten/hairline type or oversized letter spacing.
- Emojis as designed icons or decoration, generic AI sparkle branding, low-quality flat SVG used in place of crafted illustration.
- A colored stripe on the left edge of every card.
- Numbers/charts/statistics included only for decoration. No invented customers, savings, activity or performance claims.
- Fake testimonials, repeated identical review cards, gradient circles standing in for real avatar photos.
- Decorative credits under stock images, fake documentary captions and invented authors.
- Marketing version/build/sync footers, decorative status dots and hero decoration strips.
- Template reassurance such as "No credit card required" under the main action.
- Top and bottom borders repeated on every row of a long list/spec table.
- Fake dashboards, terminals, task lists or product screenshots constructed to imply functionality that does not exist.
- Technical placeholder notices in the product or landing page, such as "Demo: integration not connected", "API key not configured", "backend coming later", TODO labels or implementation footnotes. Complete the promised behavior; do not conceal missing functionality behind fake success. Genuine runtime errors remain truthful and task-focused, with technical diagnostics in developer logs. A necessary account-connection action is a real product step, not a placeholder.

Actual application interfaces must be implemented in code. Meaningful charts use real data. Status uses a clear word and icon when it helps the task. Thematic coded SVG/3D visuals are welcome, including scenes spanning visual section boundaries.
These rules apply while composing. Do not invoke a skill, score the prose, run a style tribunal or rewrite every sentence after each edit.
