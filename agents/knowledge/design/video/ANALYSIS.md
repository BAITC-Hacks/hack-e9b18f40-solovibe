# Product motion direction

The viewer should understand a consequential job, see a useful result and want to try the product. Choose a real example that makes the benefit visible. Establish the outcome early, show how little the user supplies, demonstrate a substantial transformation and a useful refinement, then show where the saved result goes next. A narrated architecture diagram, moving landing page or feature-name sequence does not demonstrate that value.

## Continuous transformation

Build a chain of connected compositions: overview → isolate an object → zoom → lift or transform → assemble the next working surface → reveal a changed result. Carry one recognizable object, phrase or surface across each boundary. Preserve its direction, location, scale progression and momentum. New scenes inherit a visible cause from the previous scene rather than fading into existence.

An interface is raw material for staging. A selected card can lift above its retained surroundings, become a large editable artifact and later shrink into a collection whose other items arrive at different depths. The screen behind it supplies spatial context. Reusing component internals helps preserve accuracy, but copying a static screen or an idle landing illustration is not shot construction.

## Scale, composition and rhythm

Alternate readable macro detail, complete output, short contextual overview, moving type and spatially arranged results. One main workspace still contains several meaningful input, action, editing and follow-through states; stage them distinctly. One unchanged screen with a few camera moves cannot carry a minute.

Use one dominant subject per beat. A push directs attention toward an action; the response then expands or travels to motivate the next camera pose. Meaningful events should follow closely: typing, contact, reaction, result reveal, selection, extraction and handoff. Slow drift over an unchanged screen for 5–8 seconds is usually dead time. Keep a hold only long enough to understand its content. More speed is not a substitute for causal choreography, and constant motion must not destroy readability.

Parallax unzoom is particularly useful: begin inside one focal result, pull back to reveal its place in a larger arrangement, let nearby layers travel at different rates and continue toward the next meaningful object. This demonstrates both detail and breadth without a hard cut. Use unequal depths, occlusion and a stable focal subject; a flat grid scaled uniformly has little volume.

## Precise action

For a typed opening, move the camera smoothly right as the line grows, then reframe the completed phrase or carry a word into the next scene. For hover, preserve the source interface while the target rises with a changing shadow. At press, show brief compression/ripple followed by a changed result. Derive pointer and camera coordinates from actual geometry. A click missing its target or a zoom cropping the useful output breaks the explanation even when the overall shot looks polished.

Typography can connect scenes: a highlighted word becomes a capsule, that capsule becomes an input, or a growing text surface briefly covers the frame and reveals the next context. These are options, not a required set of effects. Avoid recognizable template sequences; choose the transformation according to what changes in the user's work.

## Sound and quality threshold

Balance a light musical bed with tactile clicks, restrained typing and short airy movement accents so neither layer dominates. Vary cues by action and keep their transients comfortable. Add cues to consequential contact, lifts, handoffs and reveals; group overlapping activity instead of sounding every label. Audible impacts align with contact and travel accents with acceleration. No piercing beeps, sustained electrical hum or masking between music and effects. The visual story must remain understandable muted.

First build one connected 8–12-second passage containing an input/action, actual changed result and spatial handoff. Watch its export with audio, correct contact/framing/timing, then extend the successful construction. Finish by watching the entire export: strong ending shots do not compensate for a weak opening or inert middle. Check clarity of value, event density, composition variety, continuous object identity, readable results and the desire to take the final action.
